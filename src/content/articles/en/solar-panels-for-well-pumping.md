Running a well pump on solar power removes diesel costs and solves the problem of land far from the grid. The pump runs during daylight and irrigates directly or fills a tank for later. The system has 3 parts: the panels, a solar pump controller that converts the panels' direct current to drive the motor, and the pump and motor.

## Step 1: motor power

Start from the pump motor's rating in kilowatts. If you haven't chosen it yet, see [How to choose a submersible pump](/en/articles/how-to-choose-a-submersible-pump/).

## Step 2: array power

A panel's real output is lower than its rating because of cell temperature, dust and the sun's changing angle. That's why solar pumping systems are usually designed with **1.3 to 1.5 times** more panel power than motor power. The system designer sets the final figure based on the site and the controller used.

Example for a 15 kW motor:

15 × 1.3 = 19.5 kW, and 15 × 1.5 = 22.5 kW

## Step 3: panel count

Divide the required array power by the power of one panel:

| LONGi model | Panel power | Panels for 19.5 – 22.5 kW |
|---|---|---|
| LR7-72HVH | 650 W | 30 – 35 panels |
| LR8-66HGD | 610 W | 32 – 37 panels |
| LR5-72HBD | 550 W | 36 – 41 panels |

Higher-power panels mean fewer panels, structures and cables.

We supply 7 LONGi models. The main differences:

## Before you buy

- Face the panels south and avoid any shade on them during peak hours.
- Clean dust off the panels regularly, especially on desert sites.
- Choose a controller that matches the motor's power and voltage and includes dry-run protection.
- Plan a tank if you need water after sunset.
