An oversized pump raises both the purchase price and the power bill, while an undersized one delivers less water than you need or runs its motor beyond its rating. The right choice depends on 4 figures, all of which come from your well data.

## 1. Inside well diameter

The diameter of the well casing sets the largest pump that can be lowered into it. Tormac TS pumps come in four sizes:

| Well size | Pump series | Power |
|---|---|---|
| 4 in | TS 100 | 0.5 – 17.5 HP |
| 6 in | TS 150 | 1 – 85 HP |
| 8 in | TS 200 | 7.5 – 150 HP |
| 10 in | TS 250 | 12.5 – 150 HP |

There must be clearance between the pump and the well wall, because the water flowing past the motor is what cools it. ECO motors need a water velocity of at least 0.15 m/s past the motor body in the 4 and 6 inch frames.

## 2. Required flow

Flow is the volume of water per hour, in cubic metres (m³/h). For irrigation, the irrigation engineer sets it from the area, crop, irrigation method and daily operating hours. For homes and compounds, it comes from the number of units and daily use.

You also need the **well yield**: the most the well can deliver without the water level dropping to the pump. A pump that draws more than the well yields will run dry.

## 3. Total head

Total head is the vertical distance the pump lifts the water, plus friction losses and the pressure needed at the end of the network. We explain how to work it out in [How to calculate total head for a submersible pump](/en/articles/how-to-calculate-total-head/).

## 4. Power supply

Tormac ECO motors run on 380/415 V three-phase at 50 Hz, with an allowed voltage range of −15% to +6%. On a generator, its rating must cover the starting current. On solar, you need a controller made for solar pumping, and the number of panels follows from the motor power.

## From the figures to motor power

Once you know flow and head, estimating the power is simple:

**Power (kW) = flow (m³/h) × total head (m) ÷ (367 × pump efficiency)**

Example: an 8 inch well, 30 m³/h required flow, 108 m total head and a typical pump efficiency of 60%:

30 × 108 ÷ (367 × 0.6) = about 14.7 kW

The nearest standard motor above that is 15 kW (20 HP). Confirm the final choice on the model's curve in the Tormac catalogue, because each pump's efficiency changes with its duty point.

## Material

Every TS pump is stainless steel. The TS version uses AISI 304, which suits most groundwater, and the TN version uses AISI 316 for highly saline or chemically aggressive water.
