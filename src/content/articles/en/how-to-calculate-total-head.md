After flow, total head is the most important figure in choosing a submersible pump. A 20 m error can mean a pump that doesn't reach the network, or a motor larger than needed. The figure has 4 parts.

## 1. Pumping water level

A well has two levels:

- **Static level**: the depth of the water surface with the pump off.
- **Pumping level**: the depth of the water surface while pumping. It is deeper than the static level by the **drawdown**, which grows as the flow increases.

Use the **pumping level**, because that is the depth the pump lifts from while it runs. You'll find it in the well's pump-test report from drilling. The pump itself is set several metres below the pumping level so it always stays submerged.

## 2. Delivery height

The vertical distance from ground level to the highest point the water must reach: the inlet of an elevated tank, or the highest point of the land if it sits above the well.

## 3. Pressure needed at the end of the network

If the water feeds a drip or sprinkler network, the network needs a set pressure at its inlet. Each 1 bar equals about 10.2 m of head, so a network that needs 1.5 bar adds about 15 m. If the water discharges into an open tank, this part is zero.

## 4. Friction loss

Water loses some of its energy to friction against pipe walls, bends and valves. The loss increases with:

- higher flow through the same pipe diameter;
- longer pipe runs;
- a rougher bore. That's why smooth uPVC pipe loses less than steel pipe, especially after years of rust.

The exact value comes from friction tables for the diameter and flow. As a first estimate, a pipe diameter that suits the flow keeps the loss to a few metres per 100 m of line.

## Worked example

| Part | Value |
|---|---|
| Pumping water level | 80 m |
| Tank inlet above ground | 5 m |
| Drip network pressure (1.5 bar) | 15 m |
| Friction in column and line | 8 m |
| **Total head** | **108 m** |

At 30 m³/h and 108 m, you need a pump with a motor of about 15 kW (20 HP), as shown in [How to choose a submersible pump](/en/articles/how-to-choose-a-submersible-pump/).

## Total head also sets the pipe class

Column pipes carry the pump's full shut-off pressure and the weight of the column. That's why the Astral catalogue gives each class a **safe maximum pump head**. In the example, 108 m on 2 inch pipe needs a class rated above 108 m, such as Medium (up to 130 m) or higher.
