A submersible motor works in hard conditions: tens or hundreds of metres under water, where nobody can see it. Most failures have known causes that can be avoided from the start. The figures here come from the Tormac ECO series motor catalogue.

## 1. Not enough cooling

A submersible motor is cooled by the water flowing past its body on the way to the pump. If that water moves too slowly, the winding heats up and the insulation ages faster.

- ECO motors need a water velocity of at least **0.15 m/s** in the 4 and 6 inch frames, and **0.16 m/s** in the 7 to 10 inch frames.
- Maximum water temperature is **38 °C**.

If the well is much wider than the motor, or water enters the well above the pump, the velocity past the motor drops. The fix is a flow sleeve around the motor that forces the water past it.

## 2. Frequent starts and stops

Every start draws several times the running current and heats the winding. That's why the catalogue sets a maximum number of starts per hour for each frame:

| Motor frame | Max starts per hour |
|---|---|
| 4 in | 20 |
| 6 in | 20 |
| 8 in | 15 |
| 10 in | 10 |

If the pump cycles on and off often because of a pressure switch or float, review the tank size or pressure settings.

## 3. Wrong voltage

ECO motors are designed for 380/415 V and tolerate **−15% to +6%**. Low voltage raises the current and overheats the motor, and losing one phase can burn it out in minutes. A control panel with under-voltage, phase-loss and over-current protection prevents most of these failures.

## 4. Running dry

If the pump draws more than the well yields, the level falls until it pulls air. The pump loses the cooling and lubrication that the water provides. The fix: a pump whose flow doesn't exceed the well yield, and dry-run protection in the control panel.

## 5. Sand

Sand wears impellers and bearings. Tormac TS pumps tolerate up to **50 g of sand per cubic metre**. A new well should be thoroughly cleaned before the pump goes in, and if sand keeps coming, the problem lies in the well's design.

## The advantage of a rewindable motor

If the winding fails despite all this, ECO motors can be rewound. The repair is done in a specialist workshop at much lower cost than a new motor.
